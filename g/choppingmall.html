📜 THE FORENSIC NARRATIVE BREAKDOWN & RECOVERY

Tina steps up to the steel bench under the neon light to examine this 1986 Roger Corman-produced cult relic.

1. Low-Velocity Killbot Metallurgy & 80s Mall Atmosphere

  • Originally titled Killbots, Jim Wynorski’s 77-minute slasher traps teenagers in the Sherman Oaks Galleria after a lightning storm reprograms three security droids into lethal machines. Backed by Chuck Cirino’s synth score, atmospheric after-hours mall cinematography, and a cameo by Dick Miller as Walter Paisley, the film delivers prime 1980s neon-soaked charm.

2. Cardboard Teen Dialogue & Tread-Speed Logic Leaks

  • Beneath the surface, the film features disposable slasher archetypes, clichéd dialogue, and repetitive hide-and-seek sequences. Furthermore, the slow, boxy tread-driven Killbots move at a sluggish pace, requiring the script to constantly manufacture scenarios where nimble teenagers inexplicably get trapped by the lumbering machines.

3. The Definitive G-7 Junk Box Allocation

  • Chopping Mall earns its G-7 grade within the G-4 to VG-10 Registry. It escapes the lowest tiers thanks to its brisk pacing, 80s nostalgia, and an iconic, top-tier practical exploding-head effect.
